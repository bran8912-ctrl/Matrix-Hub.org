How the MPL Pro match works

🎯 3 tokens, all homeYou race three tokens. Bring all 3 to exactly 100 to win outright — overshoot (for example 98 + 4 = 102) and that token stays put on 98, because only an exact landing on 100 counts. Why it matters: late on, a big roll can be useless for one token but perfect for another.
🔄 One roll = one turn — two exceptionsEvery roll is a single turn, then play passes to your opponent — except two plays earn the same player a bonus roll inside the same turn: finishing a token exactly on 100, and knocking out an opponent's token. A move that somehow did both still earns just one bonus roll — and a bonus roll never uses up one of your 18 turns, so it is a genuinely free extra move, and it can chain if it finishes or knocks out again. Climbing a ladder does not give a bonus turn, and rolling a 6 does not give a bonus turn. Why it matters: a bonus roll is pure tempo — another chance to progress before your opponent moves, at no cost to your turn count — so finishes and knockouts are worth even more than the squares alone suggest.
↻ 1 reroll per gameEach player gets 1 reroll per game — spend it to throw away a bad roll and roll again; it doesn't use up a turn, and it can't be used on a bonus roll. Spend it after rolling, before you move or pass: the old roll is discarded, the new roll replaces it with fresh choices, and the turn is still charged exactly once, when it ends. It works only on the normal roll of your turn — it cannot be used on a bonus roll, so a bonus roll must be played as rolled (or passed) — and a reroll is not a bonus roll itself and earns nothing extra. Once spent, it is gone for the rest of the match. The Bot holds one too, and spends its own when no token can legally move with its roll or its best Coach Score choice scores below 0.
🪜 Ladders = pure climbLanding exactly on a ladder foot climbs you — nothing more, nothing less. 3→84 (+81 from the foot), 7→53 (+46), 15→96 (+81), 21→98 (+77), 54→93 (+39). Two ladders tie as the biggest climbs on the board — 3→84 and 15→96 both gain 81 squares: landing on 15 jumps you to 96, saving roughly 23 average rolls (81 ÷ 3.5) and leaving just 4 squares to finish, while landing on 3 jumps you to 84, the same 81 squares of progress from the very start of the board.
💥 Knockouts — judged on the final squareA knockout counts on the square your token finally rests on, however it got there — by the dice roll, by climbing a ladder, or by sliding down a snake. Come to rest on an opponent's token at the end of any of those and it goes back to start (0), and the knockout earns you another roll. Worked examples on this board: climb 15→96 onto a Bot token resting on 96 and it is out — 96 squares erased. Or from 49, roll a 3 onto snake head 52 and slide to 29: a Bot token resting on 29 is still knocked out — you finish 20 squares behind where you stood, but they lost 29 and you roll again, so the snake bite became a swing. Why it can beat a ladder: if their token sits on 80 and you land on it, you gain your move and erase 80 squares of their progress — a swing of 80+ squares for the price of one turn. If two of their tokens are stacked on the square, both go back — each one counts.
⏱️ 18 turns eachEach player gets 18 turns, counted on their own counter. A turn is one roll; a bonus roll earned by a finish or a knockout happens inside the same turn and doesn't reduce your count, and bonus rolls can chain. When a side has used all 18 of its turns it takes no more turns, and the match ends when both players have used all 18 — most tokens home wins; tied, total progress (the sum of all 3 token squares) decides. Why it matters: a wasted pass or an overshoot late on isn't just a lost turn — with only 18 turns each, the only way to move again without spending a turn is earning a bonus roll with a finish or a knockout.
🐍 Snakes & passingHeads: 30→11 (−19), 52→29 (−23), 70→51 (−19), 94→75 (−19), 99→4 (−95). The 99→4 snake is the cruellest square in the game — one step from home, back to square 4, losing 95 squares. You can choose to miss a turn (pass) rather than move a token onto a snake — passing uses up one of your 18 turns but can save you 19 to 95 squares.
